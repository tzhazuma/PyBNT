# Neuroanatomy Fundamentals

The human brain consists of approximately 86 billion neurons organized into
functionally specialized regions. The cerebral cortex is the outermost layer,
divided into four lobes: frontal (executive function, motor control), parietal
(somatosensory processing, spatial attention), temporal (auditory processing,
memory), and occipital (visual processing).

Subcortical structures include the thalamus (sensory relay station),
hypothalamus (homeostasis, hormone regulation), basal ganglia (motor control
and learning), amygdala (emotion processing), and hippocampus (memory
formation).

The brainstem connects the cerebrum to the spinal cord and regulates vital
functions including respiration, heart rate, and consciousness. It comprises
the midbrain, pons, and medulla oblongata.

White matter consists of myelinated axon bundles (tracts) that facilitate
communication between brain regions. Major white matter tracts include the
corpus callosum (connecting hemispheres), corticospinal tract (motor pathway),
and superior longitudinal fasciculus (connecting frontal and parietal lobes).

Gray matter is composed of neuronal cell bodies, dendrites, and unmyelinated
axons. It is found in the cerebral cortex, cerebellar cortex, and deep nuclei.
Cortical thickness varies across regions from approximately 1.5 to 4.5 mm.
