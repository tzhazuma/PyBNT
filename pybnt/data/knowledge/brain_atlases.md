# Brain Atlases

AAL (Anatomical Automatic Labeling): Contains 90 cortical and subcortical
regions (45 per hemisphere) plus cerebellar regions (116 total). Widely used
in SPM-based analyses. Regions labeled with anatomical names and numeric codes.

Desikan-Killiany Atlas: Part of FreeSurfer. Defines 34 cortical regions per
hemisphere (68 total) based on gyral and sulcal landmarks. Used for cortical
thickness and surface-based morphometry.

Destrieux Atlas: FreeSurfer alternative with 74 cortical parcellations per
hemisphere (148 total). Finer granularity than Desikan-Killiany, including
sulcal and gyral subdivisions.

Brainnetome Atlas: 246 cortical and subcortical subregions (210 cortical, 36
subcortical). Based on multimodal connectivity patterns and cytoarchitecture.

Power264 Atlas: 264 functional regions defined from resting-state fMRI
connectivity. Spherical ROIs (5mm radius). Widely used in network neuroscience
and graph theory analyses. Includes 10 functional networks.

Schaefer Atlas: Multi-resolution cortical parcellation (100-1000 regions) based
on resting-state functional connectivity. Aligned with Yeo 7/17 networks.

Yeo 7-Network Parcellation: Seven large-scale resting-state networks:
Visual, Somatomotor, Dorsal Attention, Ventral Attention, Limbic,
Frontoparietal Control, and Default Mode Network.

Harvard-Oxford Atlas: Probabilistic atlas distributed with FSL. Covers cortical
and subcortical structures. Includes probability maps for 48 cortical and 21
subcortical regions.

Brodmann Areas: Cytoarchitectonic map of the human cerebral cortex with 52
areas. Defined by Korbinian Brodmann in 1909 based on cellular organization.
Still used for functional localization (e.g., BA4 = primary motor, BA17 = V1).

MNI (Montreal Neurological Institute) space: Standard stereotaxic coordinate
space based on 152 normal subjects (MNI152). Alternatives include ICBM152
and Colin27 (single-subject high-resolution).
