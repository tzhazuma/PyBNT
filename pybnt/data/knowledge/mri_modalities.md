# MRI Modalities

T1-weighted MRI: Short TR (repetition time) and short TE (echo time). Provides
excellent gray-white matter contrast. CSF appears dark. Ideal for anatomical
imaging and volumetric analysis. Commonly acquired at 1mm isotropic resolution.

T2-weighted MRI: Long TR and long TE. CSF appears bright. Sensitive to water
content. Useful for detecting edema, inflammation, tumors, and white matter
lesions. Lesions appear hyperintense.

FLAIR (Fluid-Attenuated Inversion Recovery): Similar to T2 but with CSF signal
suppressed (dark). Excellent for detecting periventricular and subcortical
lesions. Standard in multiple sclerosis and stroke imaging protocols.

Diffusion-Weighted Imaging (DWI): Measures Brownian motion of water molecules.
Sensitive to acute ischemia within minutes of onset. Restricted diffusion
appears bright on DWI and dark on ADC maps. Key for stroke diagnosis.

Diffusion Tensor Imaging (DTI): Acquires diffusion measurements in multiple
directions (typically 30-64). Models diffusion as a tensor. Key metrics: FA
(Fractional Anisotropy, 0-1), MD (Mean Diffusivity), AD (Axial Diffusivity),
RD (Radial Diffusivity). Enables tractography for white matter visualization.

Resting-state fMRI: Measures spontaneous BOLD fluctuations at rest (no task).
Reveals intrinsic functional connectivity networks. Requires temporal
correlation analysis, independent component analysis, or seed-based analysis.

Task-based fMRI: Measures BOLD signal changes during controlled cognitive
tasks. Uses general linear model (GLM) for statistical analysis. Requires
careful experimental design (block, event-related, or mixed).

Perfusion MRI: Measures cerebral blood flow using arterial spin labeling (ASL)
or dynamic susceptibility contrast (DSC) with gadolinium contrast.
